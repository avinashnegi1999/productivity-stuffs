## 1. Foundation

Now to December 2025.

**Core skills**
- [ ] Algorithms and data structures: dynamic programming, graphs, 300+ LeetCode or HackerRank problems, structures built from scratch
- [ ] Systems: processes, threads, memory · SQL optimization, ACID, indexing · consistency, availability, partition tolerance
- [ ] Math: linear algebra (eigenvalues, SVD) · probability and statistics (Bayesian inference, hypothesis testing) · discrete math (graphs, combinatorics, logic) · multivariable calculus and optimization
- [ ] Machine learning: Andrew Ng's course · algorithms from linear regression to neural networks · bias-variance, overfitting, cross-validation
- [ ] Deep learning: TensorFlow or PyTorch · CNNs, RNNs, Transformers · vision and NLP

**Projects.** Build 3–5 high-impact projects across ML/AI, systems and data.
- [ ] AI/ML: a new algorithm with a paper, a vision app at 95%+ accuracy, NLP on real data, or a reinforcement learning game
- [ ] Systems: a distributed database with 1M+ records, a cloud-native web service, an OS kernel module, or a compiler
- [ ] Data: a real-time pipeline on terabytes, ETL at 99.9% reliability, or an insight dashboard

Aim high: an AI project with 50+ GitHub stars and a live demo, a Go, Docker and Kubernetes system at 1M+ requests, and a research project with a paper and 10+ citations.

**Competitions and open source**
- [ ] ICPC (regional or national), Google Code Jam, Facebook Hacker Cup, hackathons. Codeforces 1400+ preferred.
- [ ] Contribute to 3–5 popular repos. Build your own with 100+ stars. Record the impact.

**Research fit.** Stanford weighs research potential heavily.
- [ ] Pick 3–5 faculty. Read their 2022–2024 papers. Learn their methods.
- [ ] Write a 1-page research summary: background, projects, specific questions, current challenges.

## 2. Funding

January–March 2025.

### Knight-Hennessy Scholars

Up to 3 years of tuition, living costs and travel. $250K+ in total. About 100 scholars a year, worldwide.

- [ ] Check eligibility: first degree within the last 7 years, applying to a Stanford graduate program at the same time, top 10% of class preferred.
- [ ] Draft essays now: leadership, a global problem you'd solve, growth from failure, community.
- [ ] Prepare certified transcripts, 3 letters (academic and professional) and a CV.

Winning profiles show leadership with measurable impact, cross-cultural experience, social innovation, research potential and a personal story of growth.

## 3. Application

April–September 2025.

### Statement of purpose

Answer three questions: why you, why Stanford, why now. 2 pages max.

- [ ] **Hook, 150 words.** A real personal or technical story. No "Since childhood…".
- [ ] **Path, 200 words.** BCA as the base, self-taught CS, concrete achievements.
- [ ] **Projects, 300 words.** Your 2–3 best. Depth, measurable results, open source.
- [ ] **Stanford fit, 200 words.** Named faculty, courses, resources.
- [ ] **Research, 200 words.** Clear interests, possible advisors, PhD plans if any.
- [ ] **Goals, 150 words.** Short and long term. Industry or academia. Impact on India's tech scene.

Stress the BCA-to-CS transition and your need for funding.

### CV

2 pages, reverse chronological. Research and publications first, then projects and education.

- [ ] **Header:** professional email, LinkedIn, clean GitHub, personal site.
- [ ] **Education:** BCA with GPA if 8.5+, relevant courses, online certificates from Coursera, edX, Udacity.
- [ ] **Research:** Situation, task, action, result. Numbers, publications, teamwork.
- [ ] **Projects:** 4–5 best, with GitHub links, live demos, tech and scale.
- [ ] **Skills:** Proficiency and years, for languages, ML, systems, tools.

### Letters

3 strong letters beat 5 average ones. Use 2 academic and 1 professional.

| Who | Relationship | Speaks to |
|:--|:--|:--|
| Faculty | Research supervisor | Analytical skill, research potential, rank among top students |
| Industry | Manager or senior developer | Technical skill, real problem solving, maturity |
| Collaborator | Peer or team lead | Teamwork, leadership, character |

- [ ] Give each recommender your CV, SOP draft, accomplishments, specific examples and program details.

### Tests

Scores must be under 24 months old at the deadline.

| Test | Minimum | Competitive | Target |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| TOEFL iBT | 89 | 100+ | 110+ |
| IELTS | 6.5 | 7.0+ | 7.5+ |
| GRE, optional | | 90th percentile | Verbal 160+, Quant 170 |

- [ ] TOEFL or IELTS: 6–8 weeks of prep, register by October 2025, a practice test each week, focus on speaking and writing.
- [ ] GRE: Quant 170 (99th percentile), Verbal 160+ (85th), Writing 4.0+. Take it by November 2025.

## 5. Faculty outreach

January–March 2026. Professors get hundreds of emails. Lead with substance.

- [ ] Pick 5–7 professors. Read their latest 3–5 papers. Check who takes students.
- [ ] Write a 1-page research summary tied to their work.
- [ ] Email them with the summary and CV attached.
- [ ] Track scholarship shortlists and interviews. Apply for backup loans if needed.

**Funding on campus**
- Research assistant (RA): $50K+ stipend plus tuition. Prepare a 2–3 page proposal, a research portfolio and a strong faculty letter.
- Teaching assistant (TA), the backup: show teaching or mentoring experience and clear communication.

**Timeline:** January outreach and meetings · February RA and TA applications · March funding decisions and backups · April final commitments and visa.

## 6. Decision and departure

April–August 2026.

| Funding | Covers | You owe | Length | Bonus |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Knight-Hennessy | 100% plus stipend | Leadership activities | 3 years | Global network |
| RA | Tuition plus $50K | Research | 2–4 years | Research experience |
| TA | Tuition plus $45K | Teaching | 1–2 years | Teaching experience |
| External scholarship | Partial to full | Varies | 1–2 years | Independence |

- [ ] Read every award letter line by line: tuition, stipend, RA or TA duties, renewal rules. Add up the full cost. Find the gaps.
- [ ] Appeal if needed, before the deadline: document need, show competing offers, merit evidence, family income proof and loan letters.

**Visa**
- [ ] F-1: I-20 from Stanford International Services, SEVIS fee $350, book the interview early, passport valid 6+ months.
- [ ] Funds: bank statements showing $80K+, Stanford and scholarship letters, loan approvals, sponsor affidavit if needed.

**Housing.** Apply for on-campus housing right after admission. Waitlists are long.

| Option | Per month | Good | Bad |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Graduate housing | $1,500–2,200 | On campus, community | Limited spots |
| Shared off-campus | $1,200–1,800 | Space, flexibility | Commute |
| Studio | $2,500–4,000 | Privacy | Expensive |

- [ ] Stanford housing application with your preferences. Consider themed communities (tech, international).
- [ ] Off-campus backup: Palo Alto, Mountain View, Redwood City. Stanford Facebook housing groups. Stay near Caltrain or VTA. Budget for deposits and utilities.

**Money**
- [ ] US bank account: Chase or Bank of America. Know the wire fees. Set up mobile banking.
- [ ] Credit: get a secured card, use autopay, watch your score.
- [ ] Transfers: watch USD-INR rates, use Wise or Remitly, carry $2,000–3,000 in cash, set a transfer schedule.
- [ ] Book travel. Budget for the first 2–3 months.

**Health**
- [ ] Cardinal Care, about $5,500 a year. Required for international students. Waiver possible with equal coverage.
- [ ] Medical records translated. Vaccines: MMR, Tdap, meningococcal. A list of prescriptions. Eye and dental checkups before you leave.

## What wins

**Stand out:** depth beyond coursework · papers or talks · leading and mentoring · a unique perspective · named faculty fit · measurable impact.

**Avoid:** generic praise like "Stanford is the best" · shallow project descriptions · no named faculty · grammar errors · late submissions · generic letters.
